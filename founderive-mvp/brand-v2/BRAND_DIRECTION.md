# Founderive brand direction v2

## The business Founderive is becoming

Founderive began as a guided founder-diligence interview: answer hard questions, attach evidence, and leave with a founder brief. The product now being built is broader and more useful. It is a local-first founder workbook and system of record that connects program work, customer conversations, decisions, follow-ups, metrics, pitch versions, and milestones.

The strongest through-line is not “better worksheets” or “AI for founders.” It is this:

> Founderive turns the work of building a company into an organized, credible record of progress.

That record gives founders continuity across programs, mentors, and funders. It can later give program teams a more trustworthy view of engagement and outcomes without taking ownership of the founder's data.

## Business-model review

### Current wedge

- A free, local-first, founder-owned workbook for people starting from scratch.
- A ten-week guided track works both inside and outside an entrepreneurship program.
- Plain Markdown is the durable data layer; the app and an AI coding assistant can work on the same record.
- Founders themselves are the first users; peers in a founder cohort are the first adoption test, and program operators are a later buyer—not the v1 customer.

### Likely scalable model

Founderive is best treated as a B2B2C business with a product-led founder wedge:

1. **Founder Edition — free/open:** local app, portable record, core templates, manual exports.
2. **Founder Cloud — optional paid:** encrypted sync, backup, mobile capture, hosted AI workflows, private sharing, and richer exports.
3. **Program Edition — primary revenue:** cohort setup, curriculum mapping, facilitator workflows, consent-based founder views, outcome reporting, and capital-readiness workflows. Price by active founder or cohort, with an annual platform minimum.
4. **Implementation — secondary revenue:** onboarding, curriculum migration, integrations, and reporting setup for ecosystem organizations.

The founder must remain the owner of the record. Paid program visibility should be explicit, scoped, and revocable. Data portability is a strategic differentiator, not a feature to paywall.

### What is strong

- The founder and operator sides can create a genuine two-sided value loop: less admin for programs, more continuity and useful proof for founders.
- The product captures evidence as a by-product of real work instead of asking for retrospective reporting.
- Local-first, plain-file ownership is a credible trust position in a category full of institution-owned portals.
- Program work feeding one persistent company record is more differentiated than another course, canvas, or pitch generator.

### What still needs validation

- The current build does not yet prove who pays, how much they pay, or which outcome releases budget.
- “Everything about being a founder” is too broad for an initial category. The entry promise should stay narrow: turn weekly founder work into usable evidence and next steps.
- A local clone-and-run workflow is excellent for learning and technical founders, but too demanding for broad founder adoption. It is a wedge and trust architecture, not the finished distribution model.
- Program teams will buy for workflow consolidation, founder engagement, and defensible outcomes—not simply because founders like the workbook. A pilot must measure those three claims.
- The original diligence brief and the new workbook should not be marketed as separate products. The brief should become one output of the record.

### Recommended commercial test

Run the founder edition with 5–10 founders for four weeks, then sell one paid cohort pilot to a program. Test a fixed cohort fee before designing a complex per-seat model. The buying question is: “Will the program pay to replace fragmented homework and manual outcome chasing with a founder-owned record that produces reliable cohort evidence?”

## Positioning

**Category:** founder operating record

**Primary audience:** early-stage founders actively building through a program, accelerator, or self-paced track

**Economic buyer:** entrepreneurship programs, accelerators, ecosystem organizations, and eventually funders operating cohorts

**Positioning statement:**

> Founderive is the founder-owned operating record that turns conversations, program work, decisions, and milestones into clear next steps and credible proof of progress.

**Primary brand line:**

> Build the business. Keep the proof.

**Campaign line:**

> Turn the work into proof.

**Short descriptor:**

> Your founder record, in motion.

## Voice

Founderive should sound calm, candid, and useful. It is a capable operating partner, not a motivational coach and not an institutional compliance portal.

- Prefer: “What changed?” “Add the evidence.” “One next step.” “You own the record.”
- Avoid: “Crush your goals,” “unlock your potential,” “AI-powered success,” and vague startup hype.
- Use evidence, progress, decisions, record, and next step more often than journey, transformation, or ecosystem.

## Visual idea: the evidence trail

The new mark is a sequence of three rounded record fragments that become one forward/upward path. It encodes the product loop:

**capture → connect → prove**

The final lime segment is intentionally open and directional: Founderive does not declare a founder “complete”; it helps the founder see and substantiate what comes next.

The mark avoids the old derivative-dot metaphor. It should be understandable before it is explained, remain legible at favicon size, and work in one color.

## Palette

| Role | Name | Hex | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Primary | Record ink | `#122033` | Wordmark, navigation, dark surfaces |
| Background | Field note | `#F6F3EA` | Warm canvas; less clinical than white |
| Surface | Paper | `#FFFEFA` | Cards and documents |
| Action | Signal lime | `#CDEB52` | Forward motion, active step, confirmed evidence |
| Support | Evidence teal | `#56B8AA` | Links, sourced information, calm secondary state |
| Accent | Human coral | `#F07A68` | Attention, conversation, and people—not errors by default |
| Text | Graphite | `#252A34` | Body copy |
| Muted | Pencil | `#6E746F` | Supporting text |

Signal lime should stay scarce. If everything is lime, nothing feels like progress.

## Typography

- **Product UI:** IBM Plex Sans. Its practical, editorial character makes the workspace feel authored and operational rather than like a generic SaaS template.
- **Wordmark:** IBM Plex Sans Semibold with restrained custom tracking. The two-color construction provides the recognizable brand behavior rather than relying on a fashionable display face.
- **Page titles and key numbers:** IBM Plex Serif, set at regular weight and a compact scale. The contrast gives founder reflections and evidence a document quality without turning every heading into a display moment.
- **Numbers and metadata:** IBM Plex Sans with tabular numerals where values need to align.
- **Name construction:** set `founder` in Evidence Teal and `ive` in Record Ink on light backgrounds. On dark backgrounds, keep `founder` teal and reverse `ive` to Paper. This reveals the name's construction while retaining a calm, two-color wordmark.

The supplied SVG wordmark uses a resilient system-font stack for preview portability. A production brand package should outline the approved lettering before external distribution.

Most product surfaces are separated with rules, spacing, and alignment rather than shadows. Corner radii stay modest; pills are reserved for status, filters, and compact toggles. Elevation is reserved for genuinely floating controls such as quick log.
